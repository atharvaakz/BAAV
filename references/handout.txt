1.  Course description: The fundamental goal of an organisation is
    either to maximise shareholder value or stakeholder value, depending
    on geographical location. The globalisation of financial markets has
    forced corporates worldwide, including those in India, to focus on
    maximising shareholder value. The recent trend of consolidations,
    mergers & acquisitions, and other strategic alliances for
    sustainable growth has warranted finance domain experts from
    investment banks, investment and consulting firms, and corporate
    research wings to analyse the entire business landscape. This course
    is designed to help students interested in investment banking,
    consultancy (M&A) and equity research (investment analysis), and
    sensitises them to the various frameworks of business analysis –
    business strategy, accounting, financial and prospective – and
    valuation approaches.

2.  Scope and Objective: This course examines the role of accounting
    information and intermediaries in the economy and how financial
    analysts create value in well-functioning markets. Business
    strategy, accounting, financial analysis, and prospective analysis
    are discussed to build a better understanding and evaluation of a
    business from the perspectives of internal and external
    stakeholders. The course is designed as “Active-Based Learning” and
    uses assignment-based learning, case studies and problem-based
    learning, giving students the opportunity to apply concepts/methods
    to a real business case – analysing business performance and doing
    valuation.

3.  Course learning outcomes:

  CLO1. Sensitise students to the various frameworks of business
  analysis, such as business strategy, accounting, financial and
  prospective.

  CLO2. Critically analyse and apply various valuation approaches and
  methods.

  CLO3. Understand and evaluate the various limitations of valuation
  methods, and provide solutions.
