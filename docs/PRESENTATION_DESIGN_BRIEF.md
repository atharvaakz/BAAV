# Presentation design brief

What the research says makes slides work, and how each point is applied in `scripts/build_deck_v3.py`.

## 1. Structure: walk the audience through the work

| Principle | Evidence / source | Applied as |
|---|---|---|
| Open with Situation → Complication → Question → Answer (SCQA) | Minto, *The Pyramid Principle*; consulting practice | Slide 2: four blocks in plain words, our short answer last |
| Show the road map, then signpost every step | Signposting guides (Hamilton, UNSW, RMIT); Ausubel's advance organisers | Slide 3: numbered six-step process; a step tracker on every later slide |
| Stories are remembered better than lists | Duarte, *Resonate* ("what is / what could be"); Aaker | Each step slide says what we did, what we found, and why it matters |
| Titles alone should tell the story | McKinsey action titles; think-cell; Deckary | Every title is a full-sentence finding; read in order they form the argument |
| Lead with the thesis, then evidence, then risks | CFA Research Challenge grader guidelines; Darden; Mergers & Inquisitions stock pitch guide | Short answer on slide 2; evidence slides 5–11; conclusion and risks 12–13 |
| Keep working slides apart from presenting slides | SlideMagic; consulting practice | 13 main slides; detailed tables in the backup section for questions |

## 2. One message per slide, assertion + evidence

| Principle | Evidence / source | Applied as |
|---|---|---|
| Sentence headline plus visual evidence beats topic title plus bullets | Garner & Alley (2013), *Int. J. Engineering Education*: better comprehension, fewer misconceptions, lower cognitive load (n = 110) | Assertion title + one chart per slide |
| Remove what does not support the point (coherence) | Mayer, multimedia learning principles (18 of 19 tests) | No decorative images; one chart; short panel text |
| Do not duplicate narration word for word on screen (redundancy, split attention) | Mayer; Sweller (UNSW) | On-slide "read-aloud" text is 2–3 short sentences; the full script is in the speaker notes |
| Highlight what you are talking about (signalling) | Mayer; 2019 eye-tracking experiments on synchronised highlighting | One accent colour per company; key numbers bold in the panel |

## 3. Visual design

| Principle | Evidence / source | Applied as |
|---|---|---|
| Maximise signal-to-noise, data-ink | Reynolds, *Presentation Zen*; Tufte | No gridline clutter; light background; thin rules |
| Grey for context, one strong colour for focus; same colour for the same thing throughout | Knaflic, *Storytelling with Data*; Datawrapper; UK ONS guidance; Ajani et al. (2021) | DLF teal, Godrej ochre, Phoenix rust on every slide and chart; context in grey |
| Label directly, avoid legends where possible | PolicyViz, Flourish, think-cell, EU data-viz guide | Charts carry direct labels or short legends; panel text names the series |
| Contrast, repetition, alignment, proximity | Williams, *The Non-Designer's Design Book* | Fixed grid: chart left (8 in), story panel right (3.8 in), source line bottom |
| Readable type: sans-serif body, ≥ 14 pt for projected text, high contrast | Practical Typography; university accessibility guides (WCAG 4.5:1) | Read-aloud text 14–15 pt Calibri; titles 24–26 pt Cambria; dark ink on off-white |
| Process diagrams left-to-right, numbered, one shape style | Flowchart guides (Nulab, Creately, ConceptDraw) | Slide 3 chevrons 1→6, same shape and colour family |
| Build complex charts in layers | Schwabish, *Better Presentations* | Charts kept to one idea each; denser material moved to backup |

## 4. Delivery cues built into the deck

- A presenter can read the panel text aloud as written; the notes expand on it.
- Each step slide answers three questions: what did we do, what did we find, so what.
- Roughly 45–50 seconds per main slide for a 10-minute slot (13 slides, title and close are quick).
